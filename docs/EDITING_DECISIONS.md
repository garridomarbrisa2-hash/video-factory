# The Motivated Cut — Editing Decision Rules for the AI Director

**Purpose:** Every cut, hold, transition, text card, and beat of silence must answer
*"why?"* before *"what?"*. This is the decision brain that stops random editing.
Derived from professional editing theory (Walter Murch's *In the Blink of an Eye*)
and YouTube retention practice. Distilled into if-then rules.

---

## 1. The Master Hierarchy — Murch's Rule of Six

When a cut can't satisfy everything, sacrifice **bottom-up**. Never trade emotion
for continuity.

| Rank | Criterion | Weight | The question |
|------|-----------|--------|--------------|
| 1 | **Emotion** | 51% | How should the audience feel at this exact moment? |
| 2 | **Story** | 23% | Does this move the narrative forward? |
| 3 | **Rhythm** | 10% | Does the timing fit the pacing wave? |
| 4 | **Eye-trace** | 7% | Does the viewer's focus land where the next shot expects? |
| 5 | **2D plane** | 5% | Screen direction respected? |
| 6 | **3D space** | 4% | Geography consistent? |

**Rule:** Emotion + Story + Rhythm = 84% of a cut's value. If those work, the cut works.

---

## 2. Cut vs Hold — motivated timing

- **Cut on motion.** If a subject moves, cut mid-movement — motion hides the cut. Never cut static-to-static if you can avoid it.
- **The Absorption Test.** Hold a shot until its information or emotion is *delivered*, then cut. Info shot (b-roll/graphic): 1.5–3s simple, 3–5s complex. Emotion shot (face, pause): hold *past* delivery — emotion needs processing time.
- **The Blink Point.** Cut when the current shot's idea has been absorbed (a thought-unit completes), not mid-phrase.
- **You can't cut between branch points.** Cut at a natural beat or hold to the next one — anything between feels wrong.

## 3. VO bridges (J/L cuts) — the documentary thread

In VO-driven formats, narration is the continuous thread; picture changes underneath.

- **L-cut (default, 80%+ of b-roll):** VO continues uninterrupted while picture cuts to supporting footage. Never cut audio and video simultaneously.
- **J-cut (scene/topic transitions):** start the *next* VO line 0.5–2s *before* the visual cut. Pulls the viewer forward.

## 4. Rhythm & pacing — structure, not decoration

- **The Wave Rule.** Alternate tension and release. After a high-intensity sequence, insert a downshift (longer shots, quieter). Without release there is no tension — only monotony.
- **The Arc Rule.** Build: start medium → rise through complications → peak at reveal → short release for resolution. Faster cuts as stakes rise, slower as they resolve.
- **Length Variety.** Never 3+ consecutive same-length scenes. Healthy mix (~40 scenes / 10 min): ~50% 2–4s, ~30% 4–8s, ~15% 8–15s, ~5% 15s+ anchor holds. Uniform 3s chunks = template signature.
- **Content dictates cuts, not music.** Add music after the picture works. If cutting to music, cut 1–2 frames *before* the beat, on downbeats only.

## 5. Eye-trace & composition — where things go

- **One center of interest.** Never two competing focal points. If text appears, it IS the focus — clear the field. If b-roll has a strong subject, don't overlay text elsewhere.
- **Eye continuity.** Place the incoming shot's subject where the eye already is (brightest/largest/moving element). Break eye-trace only deliberately (to hide a plant for a reveal).
- **Lead room.** Moving/looking subject gets 2/3 of frame ahead of them, 1/3 behind. Violate only for tension.
- **Text in trailing space.** Text goes behind a moving subject (where the eye has been), not ahead (where it's going).
- **Placement:** bottom-third = names/dates/IDs/supporting facts. **Center = hero beat only** (the one number/name/thesis that IS the scene). Top third = almost never.
- **Safe zones.** All text within 90% title-safe (5% margins); mobile-heavy → 80% (10% margins).

## 6. Transition logic — which, when, why

| Transition | Signals | Use when | Mistake |
|-----------|---------|----------|---------|
| **Hard cut** (~95%) | Continuous reality | Default for everything. When in doubt, hard cut. | Almost never wrong |
| **Dissolve** | Time passed / place changed / memory | Time compression, montage, dream/reverie | Default "pretty" transition between unrelated scenes |
| **Fade to black** | End of an act | Major act break, ending (0–3 per video) | Mid-sequence, repeatedly |
| **Whip pan / punch** | Energy, momentum | High-energy sequences, related locations | Quiet sections, >2× per video |
| **Light leak / film burn** | Vintage, memory | Period/historical content, one signature style | Generic modern content |
| **Glitch** | Digital unease | Tech/crime themes only | No thematic justification |
| **Wipe** | Retro homage | Deliberate era reference | Default |

**Rules:**
- **95% Rule.** Hard cut is default. Any other transition needs a one-sentence justification. "Looks cool" doesn't count.
- **Signature Move Rule.** At most ONE stylized transition per video, used 2–4× max, always at comparable beats. More = template noise.
- **Bandage Test.** A transition covering a weak cut is a mistake. Fix the cut, don't decorate it.

## 7. YouTube retention — hooks, loops, division of labor

- **Hook (0–15s):** Pattern interrupt (0–5s, bold claim/visual, NO intro) → payoff promise (5–15s, "by the end you'll know X") → commitment hook (15–30s, plant the open loop). Steepest drop is seconds 10–20.
- **Cold open (documentary):** start at highest tension, then rewind. The tease must pay off.
- **Nested open loops.** Never resolve all loops before the final act. Main loop closes at the end.
- **Visual reset:** VO-driven → visual change every 5–10s. Plant a pattern interrupt at 0:25–0:35.
- **Division of labor:** If it can be **shown** → b-roll. **Abstract** → graphic. Must be **remembered** → text. Needs **trust/emotion** → face. Every claim maps to ONE primary visual; if you can't match a claim to a visual, cut the claim.
- **The Quiet Beat.** 1–3 bare moments per video (music drops, no text, footage breathes 3–8s): before a major reveal, after an emotional peak, at a tonal pivot. Loud beats only land against quiet ones.

## 8. Text on screen — when it helps vs hurts

- **Essential text:** numbers/stats, names, technical terms, the thesis, quotes where wording matters.
- **Redundancy ban:** NEVER transcribe VO word-for-word as text — reading competes with watching, both degrade. (Accessibility = separate CC track, not burned-in.)
- **Keyword emphasis:** 1–5 words crystallizing the point, timed to the VO. Emphasis, not transcription.
- **Reading time:** `min_hold_sec = (word_count / 4) + 1.0`. A 6-word card = 2.5s; 12 words = 4s. Absolute floor **0.85s** (≈25 frames @30fps) regardless of length.
- **Decision tree:** Shorts (sound off) → word-by-word karaoke. Long-form VO → keyword emphasis only. Hard-to-understand audio → optional CC. Must-retain verbatim → full card, held per formula.

## The Pre-Cut Checklist (run in order for every decision)

1. **EMOTION** — what should the audience feel here? (51%)
2. **STORY** — does this advance the narrative? Removed, would anyone notice? (23%)
3. **RHYTHM** — tension or release? Shot lengths varied? (10%)
4. **EYE-TRACE** — one center of interest? Where does the eye land? (7%)
5. **AXIS / SPACE** — direction and geography clear? (9%)

**If you can't articulate what a choice does, it doesn't belong.**
